# 65-day plan — 10 August → 14 October 2026

Working target: **submit 12 October**. Everything below is backwards-planned from that.

This is compressed. A well-prepared ERC application normally takes four to six months. Sixty-five
days is possible, but only if the idea is already mature and you protect writing time. Two things
sink compressed applications: (a) the host institution's paperwork arriving late, and (b) B1 never
getting an outside reader. Both are scheduled early below for that reason.

---

## Week 1 — 10–16 Aug · Clear the ground

Nothing here is writing, and all of it blocks everything else.

- [ ] **Confirm eligibility.** PhD defence date against the 1 Jan 2017 – 31 Dec 2026 window.
      Identify any extension you can claim and what documents it: leave records, medical
      certificates, clinical training records.
- [ ] **Check for resubmission blocks.** Any previous ERC submission, its call and Step 1 grade.
- [ ] **Lock the host institution.** They must formally agree to host. Contact the research /
      grants office this week: they are the ones who submit, and in October they will be doing
      this for many applicants at once. Ask them for their internal deadline — it is usually
      several days before the ERC one, and it is binding on you.
- [ ] **Request the PhD certificate** if you do not have a scan of the original.
- [ ] **Choose the panel** (and a second choice). Pull the published panel membership from the
      last two StG calls for each candidate.
- [ ] **One page on the idea.** Not a draft — the question, why it is not answerable now, and
      what changes if you answer it. This is the input to week 2.

## Week 2 — 17–23 Aug · Idea into ERC shape

- [ ] Work through `02-idea-stress-test.md` honestly. If the idea does not survive it, better to
      find out on 20 August than in April.
- [ ] Fix the **objectives**: three, occasionally four. Each independently valuable, together
      more than their sum, none of them a prerequisite for all the others.
- [ ] Draft B1 sections 1–2: state of the art and the gap; the objectives.
- [ ] Start the CV in parallel — it is 4 pages and it always takes longer than people budget.

## Week 3 — 24–30 Aug · B1 complete draft

- [ ] Full 5-page B1 draft, all sections, including the figure.
- [ ] CV and track record draft complete.
- [ ] Line up **three readers** now, with dates: one close to your field, one in your panel but
      not your field, one who has sat on an ERC panel or held an ERC grant. Ask this week —
      September is when everyone is unavailable.

## Week 4 — 31 Aug – 6 Sep · First outside contact with reality

- [ ] B1 and CV to the three readers. Give them a deadline of **13 September** and give them the
      actual evaluation criteria to score against, not a request for "comments".
- [ ] While they read: draft B2 methodology and work packages. Do not touch B1.
- [ ] **Budget v1** with the research office: personnel costs at your host's real rates, equipment,
      consumables, and whether you are claiming additional funding.

## Week 5 — 7–13 Sep · B2

- [ ] Complete B2 draft: methodology, work packages, timeline (Gantt), risks and mitigation,
      resources and budget justification.
- [ ] Every objective in B1 must map visibly onto work packages in B2. A reader at Step 2 will
      check this correspondence, and mismatches are read as a proposal assembled from parts.
- [ ] Host support letter requested in writing.

## Week 6 — 14–20 Sep · Rewrite on feedback

- [ ] Feedback in. Expect it to be worse than you hope. The most common verdict on a first ERC
      B1 is *"I can see it is good work, but I cannot see what is ground-breaking about it"* —
      and the fix for that is structural, not cosmetic. This week exists for that rewrite.
- [ ] B1 v2. Rework the opening page hardest: many panel members form their view there.
- [ ] Part A forms started in the portal.

## Week 7 — 21–27 Sep · Consolidate

- [ ] B2 v2 aligned to the rewritten B1.
- [ ] Budget final and signed off by the research office.
- [ ] Second, shorter reader pass on B1 v2 — one reader, three days, one question: *is the
      ground-breaking claim now unmissable?*
- [ ] Annexes assembled: PhD certificate, host support letter.

## Week 8 — 28 Sep – 4 Oct · Full dry run

- [ ] **Upload a complete submission to the portal.** Not a final one — a complete one. The
      portal validates as you go and finds problems you cannot predict. Submitting early and
      replacing later is standard and carries no penalty.
- [ ] Figures redrawn properly. In LS, one good schematic on page 1 or 2 of B1 does real work.
- [ ] Read B1 aloud once, end to end.

## Week 9 — 5–11 Oct · Polish

- [ ] Language pass. If English is not your first language, a native-speaker or professional
      pass on B1 specifically — it is 5 pages, it is affordable, and it is where clarity converts
      directly into score.
- [ ] Consistency check: numbers, objective names, work package names, references.
- [ ] Everything re-uploaded and validated.

## 12 October · Submit

- [ ] Submit. Confirm the submission receipt.
- [ ] 13–14 Oct held in reserve for portal failures only.

---

## Standing constraints

- **Protect 3 half-days a week minimum** for writing. This plan fails on any week where writing
  time is the flexible item.
- **The research office is a dependency, not a service.** Their internal deadline governs.
- **Do not start B2 before B1 is drafted.** B2 is invisible at Step 1, and effort spent there
  early is the most common way a compressed application ends up with a weak synopsis.
